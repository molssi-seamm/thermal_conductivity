User Guide#

The Thermal Conductivity step calculates the thermal conductivity κ from the fluctuations of the heat flux J in equilibrium molecular dynamics. By Green and Kubo,

\[\kappa_{ab} = \frac{V}{k_B T^2} \int_0^\infty \langle J_a(t) J_b(0) \rangle \, dt\]

with V the volume and T the temperature. The step reports κ in three ways, which should agree within their errors:

Green-Kubo

The plateau of the running integral of the autocorrelation function of the heat flux, fitted by one or, if justified by the data, two rising exponentials.

Helfand moments

The slope at long times of the Helfand moments, the mean square of the integral of the heat flux.

Helfand derivative

The plateau of the derivative of the Helfand moments, fitted like the Green-Kubo integral.

The heat flux must be sampled at least every 4 fs. Sampling every Δt adds the flux’s power at 1/Δt and its multiples to the Green-Kubo integral, and molecules with X-H bonds have a strong band at twice the X-H stretch, up to about 7400 cm-1 for O-H in water: 4 fs (8340 cm-1) stays clear of it, while 5 fs does not for water and 10 fs added about 20 % to the conductivity of a carbonate electrolyte. Each sample is expensive – the per-atom stress, including the long-range electrostatics, costs about two MD steps – so sampling every 4 fs rather than every 2 makes such a production run about a quarter faster. The step needs a periodic system, since the heat flux is divided by the volume of the cell.

The heat flux needs each atom’s potential energy and stress, which LAMMPS computes for its own forcefields. When the forces come from an external engine over MDI – a machine-learned forcefield or a quantum code – LAMMPS has neither, and the heat flux would hold only its kinetic term, so the step says so and skips the analysis rather than report a meaningless value.

Where the MD comes from#

The step analyzes MD that ran before it in the flowchart, or the md.h5md files of earlier runs. For independent runs, put the MD in a Loop – in parallel, with each run starting from the same structure (“Discard the structure” in the MD sub-steps) and new velocities – and analyze “the last MD run of each loop iteration”, the default. A typical flowchart:

Model Chemistry (FF@...), Packmol, minimization
NPT equilibration; Set Cell to its mean volume; NVT equilibration
Loop over the replicas (parallel)
    LAMMPS: velocities, NVT decorrelation, NVT production sampling the heat flux
Thermal Conductivity

MD to analyze chooses the runs:

the last MD run of each loop iteration

The run of each iteration of the loop around the MD, analyzed as independent runs; without a loop, the one run. This is the default.

the last MD run

The last MD run earlier in the flowchart that sampled the heat flux. One production run can feed several analysis steps.

files

The md.h5md files given, each an independent run; job://<n>/<path> reads a file of job n.

The MD must sample the heat flux at least every 4 fs: “Heat flux” in the LAMMPS NVT sub-step (the Heat Flux sub-step does too, but runs NVE). Checking the flowchart before it runs reports what is missing, with a fix that turns the sampling on. It also warns when the analysis is inside the loop holding its MD, when the loop changes the temperature or pressure (the runs are then not replicas of one state), and when the thermostat perturbs the dynamics (Langevin, Andersen) or there is none (NVE): use NVT with a Nosé-Hoover or CSVR thermostat for the production runs. Before the MD runs, the step warns if the system is small for the analysis (fewer than about 200 molecules).

Each run, and each block of a single run, must be at least 10 ps long, and much longer than the correlation time of the heat flux; a few hundred picoseconds per run is typical for a liquid.

Mixtures#

In a mixture – a solution, or a mixture of liquids – the molecules of the different species carry their energy with them as they diffuse past each other. This interdiffusion shows up in the heat flux, but it is not conduction of heat, and the Green-Kubo integral of the heat flux alone gives too large a thermal conductivity: for 1 m NaCl in SPC/E water, about 1.15 W/m/K instead of 0.81.

The step removes it following Bertossa, Grasselli, Ercole and Baroni (Phys. Rev. Lett. 122, 255901 (2019)). With e the heat flux and J the mass fluxes of all the species but one (the last is fixed by the total momentum, which is zero),

\[\kappa = \frac{V}{k_B T^2} \left[ L_{ee} - L_{eJ} L_{JJ}^{-1} L_{Je} \right], \qquad L_{ab} = \int_0^\infty \langle a(t) b(0) \rangle \, dt\]

for each direction, and likewise for the Helfand moments. LJJ is small and poorly known from a single run or block – for a dilute solution it comes from the diffusion of a few ions – so the complement of each is very noisy. The step therefore averages the matrices over the runs or blocks before taking the complement, and gets the errors by the jackknife: each run or block is left out in turn, and the values by run or block in step.out are jackknife pseudo-values, whose mean and standard error are the estimate and its error. A few hundred picoseconds per run or block is needed for a dilute solution. The LAMMPS step samples the momentum of each species with the heat flux whenever the system has more than one kind of molecule (up to 16), so nothing needs to be set. The species are the distinct molecules, by their canonical SMILES; ions are species of their own. The notes under the table say that the correction was made and give the uncorrected Green-Kubo value.

The MD must conserve the total momentum, as NVT with a Nosé-Hoover or CSVR thermostat does. If the system is a mixture but its MD did not sample the mass fluxes – MD run with an older version of the LAMMPS step – the step warns that κ is too large.

Convergence and errors#

Each method is fitted to each run, and the three diagonal components are averaged within each run. κ is the mean of these averages over the runs, and its error is from their spread: the table gives the 95% confidence interval (Student’s t times the standard error) and says so. A single run is divided into Blocks for errors from one run (5 by default), analyzed in the same way. With one run in one block there is no error estimate.

For an isotropic run – a cubic cell with nothing directional in it, such as walls or an electric field – the table gives one value per method, and a line checks that the diagonal components agree within their errors (χ²) and that the off-diagonal terms κ_xy, κ_xz and κ_yz, from the Helfand moments, are zero within theirs. Otherwise the diagonal components are given too. The values of each run or block, with the components, are in the step’s detailed output (step.out).

The step graphs the correlation functions of the heat flux (JJ.graph), the Green-Kubo integrals (GK_integral.graph), the Helfand moments (HelfandMoments.graph) and their derivatives (HelfandDerivatives.graph), averaged over the runs, with their fits.

Results#

  • κ with its standard error by each method (kappa, kappa (Helfand) and kappa (Helfand derivative)), and the diagonal components by Green-Kubo (kappa_xx, kappa_yy, kappa_zz);

  • the state of the runs: temperature, pressure, volume and density with their errors, and for runs at constant volume the equilibrium density at the target pressure when an NPT run before them gives the bulk modulus;

  • the number of runs.

κ by Green-Kubo and from the Helfand moments, with their errors, can be stored as properties; all the results can be put in variables and tables.
